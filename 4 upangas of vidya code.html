Indian Knowledge System

Exploring the rich intellectual and cultural heritage of ancient India.

The Indian Knowledge System (IKS) represents a vast collection of knowledge developed in India over thousands of years. It includes philosophy, science, mathematics, literature, medicine, arts, architecture and systems of learning.

Ancient Indian scholars developed different branches of knowledge to understand the world, human life, society and the nature of existence.

The Vedas, Vedāṅgas and Upāṅgas form important parts of this traditional knowledge framework.

INDIAN KNOWLEDGE SYSTEM

Introduction to Vidyās

Discover the traditional classification of knowledge in ancient India.

What are Vidyās?

Vidyās refer to different branches of knowledge and learning in the Indian Knowledge System. Ancient Indian scholars organised knowledge into various disciplines to develop intellectual understanding, spiritual wisdom and practical skills.

Traditionally, fourteen major branches of knowledge are known as the Chaturdaśa Vidyāsthānas. They consist of four Vedas, six Vedāṅgas and four Upāṅgas.

01 / FOUR BRANCHES

Four Vedas

चत्वारो वेदाः

The Vedas are foundational sacred texts of Indian tradition. They contain hymns, prayers, ritual knowledge and philosophical teachings.

The Four Vedas

1. Rigveda – Hymns and prayers.
2. Yajurveda – Ritual formulas and procedures.
3. Samaveda – Melodies and chants.
4. Atharvaveda – Hymns, prayers and everyday traditions.

FOUNDATIONAL TEXTS

Four Vedas

The Vedas are among the oldest sacred texts of Indian tradition and form an important foundation of Vedic knowledge.

The Four Vedas

  • Rigveda: Collection of hymns and prayers.
  • Yajurveda: Knowledge of sacrificial formulas and rituals.
  • Samaveda: Melodies and chants used in rituals.
  • Atharvaveda: Hymns dealing with everyday life and traditions.
SUPPORTING DISCIPLINES

Six Vedāṅgas

The six Vedāṅgas are auxiliary disciplines that help in understanding, preserving and interpreting the Vedas.

The Six Vedāṅgas

  • Śikṣā: Phonetics and pronunciation.
  • Kalpa: Ritual procedures.
  • Vyākaraṇa: Grammar.
  • Nirukta: Etymology and explanation of words.
  • Chandas: Study of poetic metres.
  • Jyotiṣa: Traditional astronomy and calendrical calculations.
AUXILIARY KNOWLEDGE

Four Upāṅgas

The Upāṅgas are important branches of traditional knowledge that contribute to philosophical, ethical and cultural understanding.

The Four Upāṅgas

  • Purāṇa: Traditional narratives, history and teachings.
  • Nyāya: Logic and reasoning.
  • Mīmāṃsā: Interpretation of Vedic texts and rituals.
  • Dharmaśāstra: Ethics, duties and traditional law.

01 — CULTURAL KNOWLEDGE
📜

Purāṇa

पुराण
Description

Purāṇas are traditional Indian texts that preserve ancient narratives, cosmology, genealogies, cultural memories and philosophical teachings. They explain religious traditions, creation stories and the history of dynasties.

Key Concepts
  • Ancient narratives and traditions
  • Cosmology and creation accounts
  • Dynasties and genealogies
Examples

1. Viṣṇu Purāṇa – Discusses creation, cosmology and dynasties.
2. Bhāgavata Purāṇa – Focuses on devotion and the life of Krishna.
3. Śiva Purāṇa – Describes traditions and stories associated with Shiva.

01 · TRADITIONAL NARRATIVES

Purāṇa

Purāṇas are traditional Indian texts containing stories about creation, cosmology, genealogies, legendary kings, deities and moral teachings. They preserve cultural and religious traditions through narratives.

More Information ↗

Key Concepts

  • Cosmology and creation of the universe.
  • Stories of gods, sages and legendary kings.
  • Moral values and philosophical teachings.
  • Preservation of cultural traditions.

Important Examples

  • Viṣṇu Purāṇa
  • Bhāgavata Purāṇa
  • Śiva Purāṇa

Contribution to Indian Knowledge

Purāṇas helped transmit cultural heritage, religious ideas and moral teachings through stories and narratives. They also preserve traditional accounts of society, geography and cosmology.

02 · LOGIC AND REASONING

Nyāya

Nyāya is a school of Indian philosophy primarily concerned with logic, reasoning and valid sources of knowledge. It provides methods for examining arguments and reaching reliable conclusions.

More Information ↗

Key Concepts

  • Pratyakṣa: Direct perception.
  • Anumāna: Inference or logical conclusion.
  • Upamāna: Comparison or analogy.
  • Śabda: Reliable verbal testimony.

Important Examples

  • Nyāya Sūtras by Akṣapāda Gautama.
  • Pratyakṣa (perception).
  • Anumāna (inference).

Contribution to Indian Knowledge

Nyāya developed systematic methods of logical thinking, debate and critical examination. Its approach helps distinguish valid knowledge from incorrect reasoning.

03 · TEXTUAL INTERPRETATION

Mīmāṃsā

Mīmāṃsā is a philosophical school that focuses on interpreting Vedic texts, understanding ritual instructions and examining the meaning of duties and actions.

More Information ↗

Key Concepts

  • Interpretation of Vedic statements.
  • Understanding rituals and prescribed actions.
  • Importance of duty and correct action.
  • Analysis of language and meaning.

Important Examples

  • Mīmāṃsā Sūtras by Jaimini.
  • Śabara Bhāṣya.
  • Works of Kumārila Bhaṭṭa.

Contribution to Indian Knowledge

Mīmāṃsā contributed to textual interpretation, linguistic analysis and philosophical discussions about duty, action and the meaning of Vedic teachings.

04 · ETHICS AND DUTIES

Dharmaśāstra

Dharmaśāstra refers to traditional Indian texts discussing dharma, duties, ethical conduct, social responsibilities and legal principles.

More Information ↗

Key Concepts

  • Dharma and moral responsibilities.
  • Personal and social duties.
  • Ethical behaviour and conduct.
  • Traditional legal and social frameworks.

Important Examples

  • Manusmṛti.
  • Yājñavalkya Smṛti.
  • Nārada Smṛti.

Contribution to Indian Knowledge

Dharmaśāstra texts contributed to discussions about ethics, social responsibilities, duties and traditional legal systems. They are also important sources for studying the social and legal history of India.

Contribution

• Preserves India's cultural and religious heritage.
• Transmits traditional knowledge through stories.
• Provides philosophical and moral teachings.
• Helps understand ancient Indian traditions and beliefs.

Nyāya is a school of Indian philosophy concerned with logic, reasoning and valid knowledge. It develops systematic methods of thinking, inference, debate and arriving at reliable conclusions.

• Encourages questioning and critical examination.

03 — INTERPRETATION
📚

Mīmāṃsā

मीमांसा
Description

Mīmāṃsā is a philosophical discipline that focuses on the interpretation of Vedic texts. It examines the meaning of language, Vedic instructions, rituals and the principles used to understand sacred texts.

Key Concepts
  • Vedic interpretation
  • Language and meaning
  • Ritual philosophy
Examples

1. Mīmāṃsā Sūtras – Traditionally attributed to Jaimini.
2. Śabara Bhāṣya – An important commentary on the Mīmāṃsā Sūtras.
3. Kumārila Bhaṭṭa – An influential Mīmāṃsā philosopher.

Contribution

• Develops systematic methods of textual interpretation.
• Explores the meaning of Vedic language.
• Provides principles for understanding instructions and duties.
• Contributes to Indian philosophy and linguistic analysis.

04 — ETHICS & SOCIETY
🏛️

Dharmaśāstra

धर्मशास्त्र
Description

Dharmaśāstra is a traditional branch of Indian literature dealing with Dharma, ethical principles, duties, responsibilities and social conduct. It discusses individual responsibilities and traditional legal frameworks.

Key Concepts
  • Ethics and morality
  • Duties and responsibilities
  • Social conduct and traditions
Examples

1. Manusmṛti – A traditional text discussing Dharma and social duties.
2. Yājñavalkya Smṛti – Discusses conduct, legal matters and responsibilities.
3. Nārada Smṛti – Focuses particularly on traditional legal procedures.
